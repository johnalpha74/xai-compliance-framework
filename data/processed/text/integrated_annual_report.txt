STRATE | 1 INTEGRATED ANNUAL REPORT 2025 Strate, South Africa’s principal central securities depository (CSD) and central collateral platform, plays a foundational role in safeguarding the integrity of the country’s financial markets. As a systemically important financial market infrastructure (FMI), we uphold the trust, transparency and certainty that enable market participants to transact with confidence. By securing ownership rights, enabling settlement finality and supporting robust collateral flows, we ensure that South Africa’s capital markets remain safe, stable and competitive. The year under review was characterised by heightened volatility, increasing digitalisation and evolving regulatory expectations. Against this backdrop, Strate demonstrated resilience and delivered consistently, maintaining operational excellence while advancing strategic initiatives that position the organisation for sustainable long‑term growth. Our strategy is built on strengthening our core, modernising our platforms, harnessing data as a strategic asset and building new capabilities that meet emerging market needs. Guided by our regulatory mandate and the objectives of the Financial Markets Act (FMA), we remain committed to reducing systemic risk, enhancing transparency, improving market efficiency and safeguarding investor protection. As a self‑regulatory organisation (SRO), we continue to provide the governance, oversight and standardisation required for a safe and well‑functioning ecosystem. Our partnerships remain key to our progress. Working closely with regulators, CSD Participants, issuers and other market infrastructures, we collaborate on solutions that strengthen the resilience and competitiveness of South Africa’s financial markets. Together, we continue to uphold trust while enabling the next chapter of growth through innovation. STRATE | 6 INTEGRATED ANNUAL REPORT 2025 CHAIRPERSON’S REVIEW Nigel Payne Chairperson South Africa’s economic landscape in 2025 presented a study in contrasts. GDP growth remained muted, reflecting structural constraints that continued to limit our potential. The global economic outlook remained subdued, and global tariff wars created uncertainty and elevated risk, which negatively affected business confidence, capital investment, global trade volumes, supply chains and export volumes in most countries. Yet within this subdued picture were encouraging signs: the government’s decision to reverse a proposed VAT increase demonstrated responsiveness to economic realities, while S&P Global’s sovereign credit rating upgrade – the first in nearly two decades – signalled improved confidence in the country’s reform efforts. The Government of National Unity has brought both stability and complexity to our political landscape. Coalition governance requires collaboration and compromise. We have seen progress on policy development and infrastructure reform, yet implementation remains the critical test. The Board recognises that Strate operates within a broader macroeconomic and policy context, and that the successful execution of our strategy is partly dependent on government’s ability to accelerate structural reforms and create an enabling environment for economic growth, which is essential to sustaining market confidence and activity. South Africa’s removal from the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) greylist stands as a financial sector milestone, achieved through concerted effort across government, regulators and market participants in restoring the country’s standing in global financial systems. Strate’s contribution through improving the transparency of beneficial interest records demonstrates the role critical infrastructure can play in systemic reform. Our capital markets continued to experience structural change in 2025. Although the JSE welcomed new listings, including Cell C’s return to the main board, the trend of delistings persisted. African Rainbow Capital Investments’ exit and MultiChoice Group’s delisting following acquisition by Canal+ reflect ongoing market consolidation. The Board has given considerable attention over the past seven years to understanding how these dynamics affect Strate’s sustainability and strategic positioning, reinforcing our intent to deliver core services without fail while diversifying to address revenue concentration risk. The Board has focused intensively this year on overseeing the execution of Strate’s strategic growth initiatives. These capabilities – CSD‑ to‑CSD links for global funds access and removals, collective investment scheme services, Trade Repository development, enhanced data services and innovative collateral management solutions – represent a fundamental evolution in how South Africa’s post‑trade infrastructure serves the market. Reflecting on 2025’s achievements, we see a business that has successfully protected its core while creating sustainable new capabilities. Strate 1.0, as management describes it, was fundamentally a building phase. The organisation has established the infrastructure, developed the capabilities and created the platforms that enable transformation in line with the Financial Markets Act’s objectives. Operational resilience remained non‑negotiable. Strate achieved 100% operational excellence in 2025, with no significant market disruptions. This track record of reliability, built through disciplined investment in systems, processes and people, enables everything else we seek to achieve. Our CSD Participants have similarly demonstrated stability and resilience, supporting the smooth functioning of South Africa’s capital markets through a year of both challenge and opportunity. Board composition and succession planning remain a focus area. We progressed work on reviewing our Memorandum of Incorporation to ensure it remains fit for purpose as Strate’s business model evolves. Development of a stakeholder conflict of interest management framework is well advanced, recognising the complexity that arises from our shareholder structure and expanding service capabilities. Looking to 2026, we enter what management has termed Strate 2.0 – a phase focused on achieving market adoption, scale and growth while demonstrating the value creation our capabilities enable for the broader ecosystem. The external environment for 2026 presents opportunity tempered by caution. Post ‑FATF removal from the greylist and South Africa’s sovereign rating upgrade have created positive momentum. However, constraints remain: high public debt and unemployment, infrastructure challenges and modest economic growth projections. Success depends partly on factors beyond our control: market readiness, participant appetite for change and broader economic conditions. We remain realistic about the challenges while confident in the strategic direction. Our relationship with regulators continues to be constructive and collaborative. We appreciate that robust regulatory oversight strengthens market confidence and ultimately serves Strate’s interests alongside those of the broader financial system. On behalf of the Board, I thank our regulators for their valuable guidance and engagement as we work together to maintain South Africa’s credibility as an attractive, trusted investment destination. During the year, our non‑executive directors saw some changes. Rajesh Ramsundhar, the Standard Bank shareholder‑nominated non‑executive director, was replaced by Rato de Mendonça, with effect from 1 April 2025 to 3 December 2025, at which point Rajesh was re ‑appointed to the Strate Board. Christine Clark, the FirstRand shareholder‑nominated non‑executive director, was replaced by Hendrik Fourie with effect from 10 March 2025, who has subsequently resigned from the Board effective 31 December 2025. I would like to thank the outgoing directors for their service to the Board; their insights and guidance have been invaluable. To the management team and employees led by André, I extend the Board’s appreciation for your strategic clarity, disciplined execution and operational excellence throughout 2025. The capabilities now in place represent years of focused effort. My Board colleagues deserve recognition for their constructive challenges and input, diverse perspectives and commitment to excellence in governance. Our stakeholders – CSD Participants, issuers, investors, regulators and partners across the financial ecosystem – have our gratitude for their ongoing trust and collaboration. The journey ahead requires us all to work together, embracing change while maintaining the stability and reliability that underpin market confidence. I look forward to what we will achieve together in the years to come. STRATE | 7 INTEGRATED ANNUAL REPORT 2025 CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER’S REVIEW André Nortjé Chief Executive Officer We have been on an incredible journey over the past seven years to build a sustainable business that will have a transformational impact on South Africa’s financial market ecosystem. This was Strate 1.0, and our focus was twofold: protecting and optimising our core business while establishing new capabilities that create efficiencies for the entire ecosystem. We successfully transformed a traditional central securities depository into a diversified, technology‑driven market infrastructure leader that is shaping the future of post‑trade services. This dual mandate was captured in our 2025 business theme: “Protecting our core while creating for the future” . Strate 1.0 was, at its heart, a building phase. We were laying foundations – establishing infrastructure, developing capabilities and creating the platforms that would enable transformation across the ecosystem. At the same time, we ensured that we maintained operational excellence and delivery in our core services. This achievement came against a backdrop of continued economic challenges but also significant national milestones. The formation of a Government of National Unity marked a historic shift in our political landscape, while South Africa’s G20 presidency elevated our standing on the global stage. Significantly, in October 2025, South Africa was removed from the Financial Action Task Force greylist. This was a watershed moment for our country, and I am pleased with Strate’s contribution to establishing a more transparent beneficial interest register. Enabling Strate 2.0: Scaling the market we serve Over the past seven years, Strate has delivered a material step‑change in South Africa’s market‑infrastructure capabilities. Collectively, these capabilities underpin Strate 2.0 – a strategic evolution designed to deepen and scale Strate’s role within the domestic financial ecosystem. The strategy focuses on broadening the issuer base, supported by local infrastructure, and expanding the range of assets available to investors, thereby enhancing the scale, relevance and resilience of South Africa’s capital markets. A core component of Strate 2.0 is the CSD‑to‑CSD link with Euroclear Bank, initially enabling Global Funds Access. This capability provides South African investors with local access to more than 250 000 foreign funds through a single, standardised point of entry, while the removals service enables efficient cross‑border securities movement. By facilitating access to global assets through trusted domestic infrastructure, Strate strengthens South Africa’s integration into global capital markets and supports secure cross‑border data flows. The implementation of the Trade Repository further reinforces the country’s market infrastructure. Developed in close collaboration with market participants, the repository fulfils South Africa’s outstanding G20 commitment by establishing a single source of truth for OTC derivatives data. Beyond regulatory compliance, it enhances market transparency, supports market growth and contributes meaningfully to systemic risk reduction. Strate’s Collective Investment Schemes capability demonstrates how infrastructure‑led innovation can expand both issuer reach and investor participation. By enabling transactions as small as R10, barriers to entry are reduced, while issuers benefit from improved registry, settlement and asset‑servicing efficiency. The acquisition of Trustlink and the launch of Afriverse extend Strate’s strategic footprint beyond South Africa. Engagement with the South African Reserve Bank to assess Strate’s capability to enable a critical ISO‑standard messaging network further underscores its potential role in supporting regional integration and cross‑border financial connectivity. These initiatives are underpinned by the operational Data Services platform, which establishes data as a core enterprise capability. This world‑class, AI‑enabled platform provides the foundation for clients to leverage their data to evolve business and operating models and generate deeper insights. In doing so, it reinforces Strate’s role as a trusted market infrastructure while enabling the development of new value‑adding services aligned to Strate 2.0. Finally, Strate’s collaboration with the South African Reserve Bank on a proof of capability for a triparty collateral‑management solution illustrates the system‑wide benefits of data‑enabled infrastructure. By enabling automated, data‑driven collateral optimisation, the solution has the potential to improve efficiency, resilience and liquidity management across the banking sector. Clarity of purpose An important achievement over these seven years has been creating clarity – both within Strate and across the market – about our role. We exist to establish market‑leading infrastructure that enables market stakeholders to create new businesses and transform their operating models. Through our capabilities, stakeholders can fundamentally change their cost structures and enhance their risk management. Everything we do must serve the objectives of the Financial Markets Act: increase market efficiencies and fairness, protect regulated persons, clients and investors, reduce systemic risk, increase confidence in South Africa’s markets, enhance market transparency and support interoperability and competitiveness. As a self ‑regulatory organisation, we create standardisation and finality, ensuring a level playing field where the market can operate safely. A trusted partner I am particularly proud that there are very few market ‑wide activities where Strate is not asked to participate. We have become the partner that regulators, exchange partners, Participants, issuers and investors turn to when they need to solve complex problems. This positioning as a trusted, neutral enabler was earned through consistent delivery and genuine partnership. That said, our journey has not been without challenges. Our experience implementing change across the ecosystem has taught us an important lesson: change is complex, and successful outcomes require all stakeholders working together. However, we have learned that first movers gain advantage and that the changes we introduce, while sometimes challenging in the short term, deliver long‑ term benefits for everyone. We invite our partners to walk this path with us. Our people and communities None of what we have achieved would be possible without our people. Their talent, dedication and commitment are what enable us to deliver on our purpose. We continue investing in their development, building the capabilities that will sustain our business and serve our markets into the future. Strate has held B‑BBEE Level 1 status for five consecutive years, demonstrating our commitment to South Africa’s transformation agenda. This commitment extends through our socioeconomic partnership model, creating lasting impact through infrastructure, education and skills development in communities across the country. Our sustainability approach aligns with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, spanning environmental stewardship through renewable energy adoption, social impact through community partnerships, and a commitment to good governance that underpins all we do. Looking ahead Our strategic focus remains clear: maintaining operational excellence, ensuring we have engaged and talented people, nurturing strong stakeholder relationships, leveraging infrastructure aligned with FMA objectives, and achieving scale across all our service capabilities to the benefit of the broad ecosystem. Our ongoing commitment to serving the South African financial market, combined with our proven ability to execute, gives me confidence in what lies ahead. To everyone who has been part of this journey – our Board, regulators, exchange partners, Participants, issuers, investors and, most importantly, Strate’s employees – thank you. Your dedication and commitment have been instrumental in everything we have achieved. Don’t underestimate what we have accomplished together. The momentum we have created is powerful and transformative. I call on everyone in our ecosystem to join us in this next phase. Together, we can realise the full potential of what we have built for the benefit of South Africa’s financial markets. While the country has made notable progress in some areas, significant risks continue to weigh on the business landscape. South Africa’s GDP growth is projected to be 1.1% for 2025, a modest improvement from 0.5% in 2024, yet still well below the sub ‑Saharan Africa average of 3.8%. Growth is supported by resilience in agriculture and delayed impacts from global tariffs, but remains constrained by energy insecurity, logistics inefficiencies and low fixed investment. Public debt continues to rise, with the debt ‑to‑GDP ratio expected to reach 77.9% in FY2025/26, up from earlier projections due to lower nominal GDP . Unemployment remains elevated at 31.4%, with youth unemployment at 43.8%. Structural barriers and policy uncertainty continue to hinder labour absorption. Inflation for the year was 3.6%, close to the SARB’s revised target of 3%, reflecting lower fuel and food price inflation and a stronger rand. However, volatility remains a risk amid global trade disruptions and inefficiencies in locally administered prices. South Africa’s trade relations with the US have deteriorated following the imposition of 30% reciprocal tariffs on most exports, including automotive and agricultural goods. While negotiations are ongoing, uncertainty around the African Growth and Opportunity Act (AGOA) and tariff exemptions threatens key export sectors and employment. Diplomatic tensions have intensified; however, the risk of exclusion from financial systems such as SWIFT remains low but not negligible, with trade diplomacy now a critical strategic lever. In a major milestone, South Africa was officially removed from the FATF greylist on 24 October 2025, following the successful implementation of all 22 required reforms. This is expected to reduce transaction costs, restore investor confidence and improve access to global financial systems. In this volatile and interconnected environment, Strate remains committed to proactive risk management, regulatory compliance and strategic agility. Our approach prioritises operational resilience, stakeholder trust and long‑ term value creation. We monitor macroeconomic and geopolitical developments closely, ensuring that our systems and governance frameworks remain robust and responsive. Conflicts of interest Strate is recognised locally and internationally as a financial market infrastructure and has been a key component of the financial market ecosystem for over 27 years. The company delivers services critical to the smooth functioning of the South African financial markets, as well as markets in certain other African countries. As financial markets are symbiotic, real and potential conflicts of interest exist for stakeholders, including shareholders and clients. These conflicts could generate regulatory, commercial and strategic conflicts of interest, necessitating diligent management. As Strate is a self ‑regulatory organisation, the principal CSD and the only licensed Trade Repository in South Africa, it must be perceived as independent. Any impression that Strate’s governance is unduly influenced could materially encumber its ability to deliver on its strategic imperatives. To guard Board independence and autonomy, it is imperative that the Board and management act above reproach and in line with their fiduciary duties. All directors must always act with independent minds, in good faith and for the proper purpose of the Strate Group, always prioritising its best interests with the degree of care, skill and diligence required of directors and senior leadership. To promote this concept, the first submission in each Board and committee meeting pack is an extract from common law, the Companies Act and King IV™ regarding directors’ duties, to emphasise the importance of a director’s responsibility and accountability towards the company. The Board Conflict of Interest Policy and the Regulatory and Commercial Conflict of Interest Management Policy are reviewed regularly. A good governance practice is entrenched where directors and prescribed officers declare financial or other interests that may give rise to a conflict of interest on an annual basis, as and when there are changes and at the commencement of each Board and committee meeting. Directors with a conflict of interest are recused from discussions. Where a matter for consideration is likely to give rise to a conflict of interest or a potential conflict of interest, the conflict and recusal are recorded in the minutes of the meeting, and a redacted version of the minutes is made available to the conflicted director. Board and committee packs are marked as redacted when a potential or actual conflict of interest has arisen. Directors have equal access to non‑conflicting company information and are entitled to obtain independent professional advice in line with the Board Charter. The Charter stipulates that such advice may be obtained at the company’s expense, after consulting the Board chairperson. In terms of the FMA, Strate is required to establish appropriate and effective internal governance processes to identify potential, perceived or actual conflicts of interest between its regulatory functions and its commercial services. Strate’s supervisory responsibilities are segregated from the commercial activities and overseen by the Regulatory and Supervisory Committee. Strate’s commercial services are encouraged to share information with Strate Supervision to support the supervision of CSD Participants. However, Strate Supervision may share information with the commercial services only in circumstances of potential systemic risks.

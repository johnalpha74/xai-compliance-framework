Public compliance communication 56 (PCC 56) guidance on the interpretation of Item 3 of Schedule 1 to the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001 (Act 38 of 2001) Page 2 of 10 PCC SUMMARY Item 3 of Schedule 1 of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act , 2001 (Act 38 of 2001) (FIC Act) refers to the definition of an estate agent as defined in the Estate Agency Affairs Act, 1976 (Act 112 of 1976) (EAA Act). The EAA Act was repealed and replaced by the Property Practitioners Act, 2019 (Act 22 of 2019) (PP Act). The definition of “estate agent” has been replaced by “property practitioner”. The definition of property practitioner is wider than estate agent. The PP Act read together with section 12 of the Interpretation Act, 1957 (Act 33 of 1957) indicates that reference to “estate agent as defined in the Estate Agency Affairs Act” includes “property practitioners as defined in Property Practitioners Act” limited to the activities as defined in the now repealed definition of an estate agent. Practically, this means that any person registered as an estate agent prior to February 2022 will remain an accountable institution in terms of Item 3 of Schedule 1 to the FIC Act. Any person performing the activities of an estate agent, although they may be referred to as a property practitioner after February 2022, will too be considered as an accountable institution in terms of Item 3 of Schedule 1 to the FIC Act. RELEVANT LEGAL FRAMEWORK REGARDING PROPERTY PRACTITIONERS IN SOUTH AFRICA AND THE APPLICATION OF THE FIC ACT 1.1. Item 3 of Schedule 1 to the FIC Act includes an estate agent as an accountable institution as defined in the Estate Agency Affairs Act, 1976 (Act 112 of 1976) (EAA Act). Annexure A provides an excerpt listing the full definition of an estate agent in terms of the EAA Act. 1.2. The EAA Act was repealed by the Property Practitioner s Act, 2019 (Act 22 of 2019) (PP Act) with effect from 1 February 2022. This resulted in replacing the term “estate agent” with the term “property practitioners” which includes additional categories of persons. Refer to Annexure B for the full definition of property practitioner in terms of the PP Act. 1.3. The P P Act read together with section 12 of the Interpretation Act , 1957 (Act 33 of 1957) indicates that when interpreting Item 3 of Schedule 1 to the FIC Act, the reference to “estate agent as defined in the Estate Agency Affairs Act ”, now includes “property practitioners as defined in the Property Practitioners Act” limited to persons who perform activities as defined in the now repealed definition of estate agent. 1.4. Practically, this means that any person registered as an estate agent prior to February 2022 will remain an accountable institution in terms of FIC Act. Any person performing the activities of an estate agent , although they may be referred to as a property practitioner after February 2022, will too be considered as an accountable institution in terms of Item 3 of Schedule 1 to the FIC Act. 2. REGISTRATION OBLIGATIONS IN TERMS OF THE FIC ACT 2.1. All property practitioners who perform “estate agent” activities fall within the ambit of Item 3 of Schedule 1 to the FIC Act and must register as accountable institutions with the Centre, in the manner as set out in public compliance communication 5C (PCC 5C) as published on the Centre’s website.

BROADER CATEGORY OF PROPERTY PRACTITIONERS 3.1. The PP Act defines a property practitioner widely, which includes the traditional “estate agents”. I n addition, the term “ property practitioner ” includes certain auctioneers, m anaging agent s, business brokers, trust ees, bond or mortgage originators and bond br okers, property developers who market or sell properties, persons who market or sell timeshare or fractional ownership, and homeowners associations. 3.2. The broader category of persons , who are now known as property practitioners, do not automatically become accountable institutions unless the person performs an activity which is equivalent to that of an “estate agent” as envisioned in the now repealed EAA Act. 3.3. In order for the full ambit of property practitioners to be regarded as accountable institutions, Schedule 1 to the FIC Act will have to be amended to include “property practitioners” in its entirety. 4.
